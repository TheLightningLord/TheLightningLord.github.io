Why We Train: The 6 Pillars

🩸

Type 2 Diabetes

Muscle contraction moves glucose from the blood into cells without insulin via GLUT4 translocation. Walking after meals can reduce blood sugar spikes by up to 22%.

Mechanism: Non-insulin mediated glucose uptake.
🧠

Cognitive Function

Aerobic exercise triggers the release of BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor), which acts like "fertilizer" for new neurons in the hippocampus (memory center).

Mechanism: Neurogenesis & Increased Hippocampal Volume.
⚖️

Fall Prevention

Falls are often due to a lack of power (speed of movement). Power training reduces fall risk by improving the reactive step strategy needed to catch yourself.

Mechanism: Type II Fiber Recruitment & Proprioception.
❤️

Premature Mortality

Just 150 minutes of moderate activity per week is associated with a 31% reduction in all-cause mortality risk compared to sedentary individuals.

Source: Arem et al., JAMA Internal Medicine (2015).
🦴

Bone Density

Bones respond to force (Wolff's Law). Axial loading (weights) stimulates osteoblasts to lay down new bone matrix, combating osteoporosis.

Mechanism: Mechanotransduction via Osteocytes.
🏡

Independence (ADLs)

Strength correlates directly with the ability to perform Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) like toileting, dressing, and rising from a chair unassisted.

Outcome: Reduced risk of institutionalization.

Evidence-Based Supplementation

We focus only on supplements with strong clinical data for aging adults. Always consult your physician before starting new supplements.

Grade A Evidence

Creatine Monohydrate

Why: Often misunderstood as just for bodybuilders. In seniors, creatine helps retain muscle mass, improves cognitive function, and may support bone health.

Dose: Typically 3-5g daily. No loading phase needed.

Source: Chilibeck et al. (2017) - Effect of creatine during resistance training in older adults.
Essential

Vitamin D3 + K2

Why: Critical for calcium absorption. Deficiency is linked to higher fall risk and fractures. K2 ensures calcium goes to bones, not arteries.

Dose: Depends on blood work. Common is 1000-2000 IU daily.

Source: Bischoff-Ferrari et al. (2009) - Fall prevention with supplemental and active forms of vitamin D.
Anti-Inflammatory

Omega-3 (Fish Oil)

Why: Supports brain health and combats anabolic resistance (helping your muscles use protein better). Lowers systemic inflammation.

Dose: Look for high EPA/DHA content (approx 2g combined).

Source: Smith et al. (2011) - Omega-3 supplementation augments muscle protein synthesis.
Sleep & Muscle

Magnesium Glycinate

Why: Many seniors are deficient. Helps with muscle cramps, sleep quality, and blood pressure regulation.

Dose: 200-400mg before bed.
