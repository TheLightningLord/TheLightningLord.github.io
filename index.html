Evidence-Based Clinical Outcomes

We utilize targeted interventions for the specific physiological challenges of aging. Select a condition to review the clinical data.

🩸 DIABETES MELLITUS
▼

Mechanism of Action

GLUT4 Translocation: Muscle contraction acts as "invisible insulin," moving glucose from the blood into cells without insulin. This effect persists for up to 48 hours.

Risk -42%
Source: Sigal et al., Diabetes Care (2006)
🦵 OSTEOARTHRITIS
▼

Mechanism of Action

Synovial Fluid Dynamics: Movement creates a "sponge effect," pumping nutrient-rich fluid into the joint capsule to lubricate cartilage. Mechanical loading is superior to rest for pain management.

Pain -40%
Source: Moseng et al., BJSM (2017)
⚖️ FALL RISK REDUCTION
▼

Mechanism of Action

Type II Fiber Recruitment: Falls occur due to a lack of power (speed). Training fast-twitch fibers improves the "reactive step" needed to catch balance during a slip.

Risk -34%
Source: Sherrington et al., Cochrane Database (2019)
🧠 COGNITIVE DECLINE
▼

Mechanism of Action

BDNF Release: Aerobic activity triggers Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor, which promotes neurogenesis in the hippocampus (memory center), effectively reversing age-related shrinkage.

Volume +2% (Reverses Atrophy)
Source: Erickson et al., PNAS (2011)
❤️ HYPERTENSION
▼

Mechanism of Action

Post-Exercise Hypotension: Shear stress on arterial walls releases Nitric Oxide, a potent vasodilator. This relaxes blood vessels and lowers resting blood pressure significantly.

BP -8mmHg
Source: Pescatello et al., ACSM (2004)
💪 SARCOPENIA
▼

Mechanism of Action

Anabolic Signaling: Resistance training activates the mTOR pathway, stimulating muscle protein synthesis even in advanced age (90+), preserving functional independence.

+113% Strength
Source: Fiatarone et al., JAMA (1990)
